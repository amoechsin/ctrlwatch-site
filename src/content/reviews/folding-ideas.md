Industries do not, as a rule, answer YouTube videos. They ignore them, or they send a lawyer. In January 2022 Dan Olson uploaded "Line Goes Up – The Problem With NFTs," a 137-minute essay, and the industry it described did something industries rarely do with a video essay: it answered. Time, Nasdaq, Decrypt and Cointelegraph all felt obliged to respond, and millions of people watched the thing they were responding to. CTRL+WATCH's Essay Issue put the claim in print and stands by it: "Line Goes Up" may be the most consequential single essay the form has produced — the strongest evidence yet that a YouTube upload can function as a public document, entered into the record once and argued with by the people it names.

Then the second number, and the review has to hold both. Between September 2023 and July 2026, the public Folding Ideas channel showed — as far as anyone outside its Patreon can verify — one new video. Folding Ideas scores 84. It does not enter the [Top 50](/top50/). That is the hard argument of this review, and we intend to own it rather than apologise for it.

#### What It Does

Olson is Canadian, works out of Calgary, and has published as Folding Ideas since the early 2010s. The channel's roots are in film and media criticism, but over the years it found a larger subject: belief — specifically, how communities talk themselves into things the evidence won't support. "In Search of a Flat Earth" (2020) is the template. Olson went to Lake Minnewanka and ran an actual curvature experiment, the essayist doing the fieldwork rather than citing someone who did, and then pivoted into the origins of flat-earth belief and the QAnon-adjacent conspiracy culture it bled into. "Line Goes Up" turned the same method on a market. "This Is Financial Advice" (September 2023) turned it on meme-stock culture. Read together they form an unofficial trilogy about what happens when a crowd decides the facts are negotiable, and nobody on the platform has written that subject better.

#### What It Does Extraordinarily Well

The Content Quality at 95 is earned by method more than by polish. Olson takes the promise at its word and tests it: if the earth is flat, go to the lake and look; if NFTs solve these problems, walk through the problems one by one. The deadpan delivery — dry to the point of geology — lets the evidence carry the comedy, and there is a great deal of comedy in the evidence. Replay Value at 91 reflects the rarest thing an essay about a news event can do, which is outlive the news; the flat-earth video plays better today as a field guide to conspiracy thinking than as a report on one community. X-Factor 90 prices the credibility no production budget can buy: the standing to be answered by the people you're criticising.

> "Line Goes Up" made an industry answer a YouTube video. The Top 50 ranks the channel that came after — and for two and a half years, as far as YouTube could see, that channel said nothing back.

The fair critique of the masterpiece deserves its paragraph. Critics responded that "Line Goes Up" proves the promised use cases fail without spending much time on why people actually bought in — that it answered the brochure rather than the buyer. Coming from a channel whose great subject is belief, that is a pointed miss, and it is the reason the Content Quality stops a notch short of the very top of the scale.

#### Where It Falls Short

The 84 is a calendar verdict, and the 38 sets it. In Issue #018 we held [OverSimplified](/reviews/oversimplified/) out of the Top 50 on the same principle and said so plainly: a calendar problem no drop can solve. Folding Ideas gets the same treatment, with the same respect. The economics deserve naming fairly, because they are not a vice. The most recent essay, "Belle and the Meaning of Place," went to patrons in March 2026 and reached YouTube on 7 July. That is the room-of-one's-own arrangement the Issue #020 Cheat Codes feature describes, working exactly as designed: the people paying the rent get the key first. Nothing about it is hoarding. But the Top 50 ranks a public channel's present-tense presence on the platform, and for two and a half years that presence was, from the YouTube side, a locked door with a bell on it. Community at 78 is the same fact seen from the far side of the paywall — a thriving room inside, a waiting street outside.

**The verdict.** EXCELLENT, 84, and into the 84 waiting room alongside [Coffeezilla](/reviews/coffeezilla/), the platform's other great chronicler of financial delusion, and seven others, [F.D Signifier](/reviews/f-d-signifier/) among them — nine deep, the fullest that room has ever been. If the verdict feels wrong, good. It should. A ranking that let one masterpiece outvote the calendar would be a ranking of videos, and you already keep one of those. It's called your Watch Later.

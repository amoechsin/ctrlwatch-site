The Anglosphere has a bad habit of measuring YouTube history by how it looks in English, and it keeps producing the same wrong conclusion: that the serious stuff is a niche, that education at scale needs a comedy wrapper or a documentary budget to survive. Benjamin Brillaud has spent since 2014 quietly disproving it in French. Nota Bene is the largest French-language history channel on the platform, ~2.7M subscribers deep, and it is neither a comedy engine nor a prestige documentary house. It is a man explaining history clearly, ten to twenty minutes at a time, more often than almost anyone should be able to sustain — and the subscriber count says the audience was there all along, waiting for someone to talk to them in their own language at that register.

#### What It Does

The format is deliberately unglamorous and enormously effective: accessible episodes, a genuine curiosity across eras and cultures, and a willingness to reach outside the well-trodden Western canon. The output is the headline stat. Season 12 (2025) ran seventy-three episodes; Season 13 opened in 2026 with nineteen more between January and March. That is not a channel dabbling in consistency — it is a channel that has made reliability its identity. When a summer-2025 episode on the Shroud of Turin broke into the mainstream French press, it was not a fluke of virality; it was the occasional visible tip of a machine that has been running properly for a decade.

The Consistency at 95 is the plain reading of that cadence. In a genre where the prestige tier measures its output in episodes per year and often means it literally, Brillaud measures his in episodes per season, plural, dozens at a time, without the quality collapsing into filler.

#### What It Does Extraordinarily Well

The X-factor here is institutional, and it is one no Anglophone equivalent can currently claim. Nota Bene is one of nine creators in the Bibliothèque nationale de France's RetroNews project, making videos drawn from three centuries of the BnF's press archive. Sit with what that means. The national library of France handed a YouTuber the keys to its historical newspaper collection because it decided he was a serious enough educator to be trusted with it. That is not a sponsorship. It is a moat — an institutional endorsement and a source of primary material that no amount of production budget can replicate, because you cannot buy the BnF, you can only be invited in.

It is the kind of legitimacy Anglophone history YouTube keeps trying to manufacture — the archival deep-dive, the primary-source flex — and Brillaud simply has, granted rather than performed. The X-Factor at 76 reflects a channel whose intangible is not a personality tic but an institution's trust.

> Serious history education does not need English to reach millions. Nota Bene has been the proof for a decade, and the Anglosphere simply wasn't counting.

#### Where It Falls Short

Where it stops short is the honest floor-versus-ceiling question that seventy-three episodes a year forces. Replay Value at 72 is the tell: a channel this prolific, this accessible, is optimised for the first watch and the broad reach, not the return. These are excellent explainers you learn from once, more than treasures you revisit — the price of a floor that never drops is a ceiling that rarely soars into the rewatchable. Nobody rewatches a Nota Bene episode the way they reread a chapter; they watch it, they learn the thing, they move to the next.

That is not a failure — it is the deliberate trade of a channel built for reach and reliability rather than for the singular, revisitable object. But the score has to price it, and 72 is the price.

**The verdict.** At 82 it lands below the 84 Top 50 threshold, which is the correct outcome and not a slight: this is a superb, load-bearing channel for an entire language's history education, with one honest limitation, and the number reflects exactly that. The standing commitment to non-English YouTube — Dot CSV in [Issue #017](/issues/017/), Choque de Cultura in [Issue #016](/issues/016/) — continues here, and it continues because channels like this one earn the column, not to fill a quota. EXCELLENT, 82.

There is no narration. That is the first thing to say about Li Ziqi, and in a review written in a language her channel has never needed, it may be the most important: rain on a tile roof, a fire catching, a blade through bamboo, her grandmother being called to eat. The videos — rural Sichuan, traditional craft and cookery filmed with a patience most documentaries can't afford — simply show a woman making things from the ground up: soy sauce from the bean, silk from the worm, furniture from the grove. Nothing is explained. Everything is shown. It is the maker video reduced to its oldest possible form, which is watching skilled hands and keeping quiet, and it has carried further than any talking head on this platform: Guinness verified her in January 2021 as the most-subscribed Chinese-language channel on YouTube, at 14.1 million. Trackers now put the figure at roughly 30 million. The numbers conflict by source and date, and we print the hedge — but every one of them is a record.

This profile is the magazine's standing non-English commitment, issue nine of it. It is also something more, and we'll say it plainly before the quibbles: Li Ziqi enters the [Top 50](/top50/) at #30 — the first non-English-language channel ever to hold a seat on this table. Karim A. of Cairo asked us in #020 whether non-English coverage here was a window or a token. This is the structural answer. The window is now a chair.

#### The silence, and what it was

Grade the arc honestly and the first thing you hit is the gap. Her last pre-hiatus video went up in July 2021, and then: nothing, for three years and four months. On most channels that silence reads as burnout, drift, or the quiet death of a format. Hers was none of these. In October 2021 her company sued Hangzhou Weinian, the MCN that held 51% of their joint venture to her 49% — a fight, in plain terms, over who owned the name "Li Ziqi" and the business built on her hands. Suits and countersuits followed; in late December 2022 a court-mediated settlement, reported by TechNode, China Daily and the South China Morning Post, left her holding 99%. Terms undisclosed. She came back on 12 November 2024 with two videos — a lacquered wardrobe, a bamboo forest closet — and the return registered as a seismic event: over 100 million views on Weibo within hours by Yicai's and Jing Daily's accounts, millions more on YouTube in the first day, with every outlet reporting a different astonishing number and all of them astonishing. Three years of silence, and the audience was simply there, undiminished, as if holding a seat.

So the Consistency score of 35 — the lowest ever attached to an EXCELLENT verdict in this magazine — needs its footnote in full view. It prices the three-year silence and a post-return cadence we could not verify: no written source we trust lists her 2025–26 upload calendar, and we could not read the channel page directly at press time. We say that in print rather than pretend otherwise. But the silence itself was litigation, not lethargy — a creator spending her consistency to buy her own name back. She lost the calendar and won the company. This table has priced worse trades at higher scores.

#### What the 96 and 97 are for

Content Quality at 96 is the easiest number in the issue to defend. The craft is real — the processes are long, seasonal, and genuinely performed, a single video sometimes spanning months of planting, harvesting and making — and the cinematography has been credited by outlets across a dozen countries for carrying the work across every language barrier on earth. Replay Value at 94 follows: an archive with no news hook, no discourse, and no dialogue does not age, and hers plays today exactly as it did in 2019. The closest relative on this table is [Primitive Technology](/reviews/primitive-technology/) at #4 — the other wordless maker whom the whole world watches build — and she is the only channel we have ever reviewed that belongs in that sentence. The X-Factor at 97 is the simplest: there is one of her. An entire genre of imitators exists, and the China Project put the problem in a headline when she returned — can she beat her copycats? — and the answer so far has been that the copies prove the original.

Two controversies belong in the record, both printed evenhandedly. Her idyll sits inside a soft-power argument: state media has praised her as a model cultural export while critics abroad read the same videos as a scrubbed, government-convenient picture of rural China — VOA quoted the blunt version, that visibility at her scale implies official blessing. And in January 2021 a video of pao cai, Sichuan pickled vegetables, tagged #ChineseFood, drew furious objections from Korean netizens amid a wider, ISO-adjacent dispute about kimchi's provenance. We note both without pretending to adjudicate either; a channel this large, this quiet, and this beautiful will be argued over by governments, and the videos go on saying nothing.

#### Where It Falls Short

Community at 82 reflects what we can see from outside: a global audience of staggering scale whose conversation happens mostly on platforms we can't grade and in languages we won't pretend to have read. And the honest ceiling on the whole exercise: we reviewed an archive and an arc. If the return turns out to be a coda rather than a second act, the Consistency axis has already said so.

**The verdict.** EXCELLENT, 87, NEW at #30 — above channels with twenty times her output, on the strength of the only two axes no calendar can touch. [Al-Daheeh](/reviews/al-daheeh/) opened the door last issue; Li Ziqi walks through it carrying a basket of her own silk. The Top 50 waited twenty-one issues for a channel that doesn't speak English. It got one that doesn't speak at all.

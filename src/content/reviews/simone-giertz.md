Begin with the alarm clock that slapped her awake with a rubber arm. The toothbrush helmet. The chopping machine that mostly attacked the vegetables' general neighbourhood. For about five years, Simone Giertz was the self-crowned — technically subreddit-crowned — Queen of Shitty Robots, a Swedish ex-physics student building machines whose entire job was to fail on camera, delightfully, in front of millions. It would have been easy to file her under comedy and move on. It would also have been the single worst read of a creator this platform has produced, because the robots were never the joke. They were an apprenticeship, conducted in public, with the audience invited to every lesson.

She said as much herself, on one of the bigger stages a maker has been handed: her 2018 TED talk, "Why you should make useless things," which turned the bit into a philosophy. Build something with no stakes and you can't fail at it; remove the fear and the learning gets in anyway. That idea is the spine of this entire issue, and Giertz is its clearest living demonstration — which is why the Maker Issue starts its Player Profiles here.

#### What It Does

The channel today is a different machine from the one the subreddit crowned. The first video, by the best account we can source, was a popcorn catapult in September 2014. The decade since runs: shitty robots; a benign brain tumour, revealed in April 2018 and nicknamed Brian, removed that May; the January 2019 announcement that parts of it remained and radiation was next — five days a week, six weeks, documented with the same unflinching good humour she pointed at her own robots. And then the pivot that makes her this issue's thesis made flesh: from machines designed to fail toward products designed to ship. Truckla, the Tesla Model 3 she and a small crew converted into a pickup truck in June 2019 — years before Tesla shipped anything with a bed on it — was the hinge: a "shitty robot" in scale and audacity, except it worked. After that came the Every Day Calendar, a Kickstarter that she has said raised over half a million dollars against a $35,000 goal, and eventually a shelf in MoMA's shop; then Yetch, the product-design company she launched in 2022; and in 2026, a Kickstarter for a laundry chair — a chair designed for the clothes that are neither clean nor dirty — reported to have raised roughly $966,000 against a $50,000 goal. That figure comes from aggregator coverage and we print it as reported; the nineteen-times-over shape of it is not in dispute.

#### What It Does Extraordinarily Well

The X-Factor at 93 is the voice, and the voice is the rarest kind on YouTube: a person constitutionally incapable of pretending competence she hasn't earned yet, who turns that honesty into engineering pedagogy. Watch any build and the failure arrives on screen un-edited — not as a blooper reel, as the content itself. The Community score of 88 is the direct product: an audience that has followed her from joke machines through brain surgery to furniture, because the thing they subscribed to was never robots. It was the permission to be a beginner in public.

Content Quality at 88 prices the craft of the storytelling as much as the builds. A Giertz video is structured like an essay about a problem — the laundry chair began as a video about the clothes-chair problem every human bedroom shares — and the product emerges as the argument's conclusion. Nobody else on the platform walks the line between workshop and design studio with this much charm and this little self-congratulation.

#### Where It Falls Short

The honest critique: the pivot traded chaos for polish, and some voltage went with it. A product company runs on timelines, tooling and quality control, and those are quieter stories than a toothbrush helmet; the cadence is roughly monthly now, and the products visibly compete with the videos for her year. Replay Value at 82 reflects a catalogue whose early era and current era are almost two different channels — brilliant on both sides of the line, but you can feel the line. Consistency at 74 is the schedule of a person who also runs a company, priced without resentment.

**The verdict.** EXCELLENT, 86, NEW at [#43 in the Top 50](/top50/) — entering in the same issue as the Boss Fight between [Mark Rober](/reviews/mark-rober/) and [Stuff Made Here](/reviews/stuff-made-here/), which puts three of the platform's defining engineer-creators on the same page and lets us say plainly where she fits: Rober industrialised the spectacular build, Wighton weaponised the impossible one, and Giertz did something neither has quite managed — she made the failing itself the product, then proved the method by shipping real ones. The window seat in this issue belongs to someone else. The thesis belongs to her.

Start with an admission, because the readers who remember Issue #011 have earned it: we dropped this channel for a crime it didn't commit. Summoning Salt left our Top 50 at 82, reason logged as "displaced" — not a quality complaint, just crowding at the bottom of the table in a busy issue. Three years of accumulated evidence have made that displacement indefensible, and the Competition Issue is the only honest place to say so. Nobody on this platform — nobody — documents competition itself better than a man you almost never see, narrating the decades-long wars over hundredths of a second in games older than most of the people fighting them.

#### What It Does

The format has not changed since the early days, which is precisely the point; it has *deepened*. A World Record Progression video takes one game — Mike Tyson's Punch-Out!!, the original Super Mario Bros., "The History of Minus World Speedruns" (December 2024), "The History of 120 Star's Early Era" (April 2025) — and tells the history of its record the way a war historian tells a campaign: era by era, runner by runner, heartbreak by heartbreak, over the synth swell of HOME's "We're Finally Landing," an intro so ritualised that the first chord now functions as a starting pistol. What has changed is scale. The early videos ran twenty minutes; the recent work runs feature-length, closer to two hours, each one effectively a documentary film researched out of forum archives, dead YouTube accounts, and Twitch VODs that this channel is often the only institution bothering to preserve.

That last clause is the part the casual read misses. Speedrun history lives in the most perishable archive on the internet — decade-old forum threads, deleted accounts, unlabeled VODs — and these films are frequently the only durable record of communities' entire competitive eras. He is not a gaming channel that mentions history. He is a sports historian whose sport happens to be Nintendo, and the primary sources are rotting faster than anyone else is reading them.

#### What It Does Extraordinarily Well

Structure, first. Every episode is built on the same engine — a record that looks final, a community that refuses to believe it, and the slow escalation from optimisation to obsession — and the engine never tires, because the stories are genuinely different people breaking in genuinely different ways. The Content Quality at 93 prices research depth most documentary channels never attempt; the Replay Value at 94 prices the fact that these films outlive their own records. The times fall. The stories don't. You rewatch a World Record Progression the way you rewatch a good sports documentary: knowing the ending, for the middle.

And then there is the thing that separates the chronicler from the commentator: he plays. In February 2025, after five years and by his own count roughly seventy-five thousand attempts, he broke the two-minute barrier on Punch-Out!!'s final fight — a record he had spent years narrating other people's failures to reach. Read that again. The historian of the impossible spent half a decade inside his own genre's plot and emerged holding the record. It is as if Herodotus, mid-chronicle, had put down the stylus and won the battle of Marathon. That is what the X-Factor at 93 is made of: an entire genre, now widely imitated, with exactly one canonical voice — and the voice has standing.

> Speedrunning never had an esports winter, and Summoning Salt is the reason you understand why: it was never a business. It was a priesthood, and he is its chronicler.

#### Where It Falls Short

The calendar, and we have been here before with Clickspring (88, Consistency 62) and Fall of Civilizations (89, Consistency 58): Consistency at 62, videos arriving like comets, months of silence between uploads that are each two hours long. The one public glimpse of the man himself — a June 2025 face reveal, after eight years, staged to sell a limited-run VHS tape of his own video, a physical artifact for the archive about archives — tells you everything about the pace. This is a person who would rather be right than frequent, and the table has a number for what that costs, even when the trade is correct.

**The verdict.** Re-evaluated 82 → 88, six points, and back into the [Top 50](/top50/) at #23 — the first displacement this magazine has formally reversed. The apology is the ranking. Watch the Punch-Out!! history first, then understand that the man narrating it now holds the record it's about.

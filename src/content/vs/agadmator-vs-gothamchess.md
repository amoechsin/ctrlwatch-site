Before October 2020, chess YouTube had a king, and it was a soft-spoken Croatian named Antonio Radić who filmed a chessboard, a captured-pieces tray, and nothing else. agadmator was the first chess channel to a million subscribers, the biggest in the world from 2018 onward, built one daily historical game at a time. Then Netflix aired The Queen's Gambit, a pandemic-bored planet discovered that chess.com existed — a million new accounts a month, at the peak — and the boom needed something the archive could not supply: a broadcaster. It found Levy Rozman, an International Master from New York who could make a tournament recap feel like a heavyweight presser, and by late 2021 GothamChess had taken the crown. Today Levy sits near eight million subscribers; agadmator holds around 1.35 million, roughly where the boom left him. The market has spoken. The market, however, does not get to write this magazine, which is why the fight is worth staging.

Because this is the cleanest possible test of a question the whole Competition Issue circles: when a game becomes a spectator sport, what actually gets rewarded — the game, or the coverage of the game? agadmator is the archivist: Morphy, Tal, Fischer, the immortal games, narrated with the same layout and the same gentle cadence since 2016, a museum that happens to upload daily. GothamChess is the broadcaster: recaps, drama, Guess the Elo, the Carlsen–Niemann scandal called like a title fight days after it broke. One of them kept the flame. One of them caught the fire.

| Tale of the Tape | agadmator | GothamChess |
|---|---|---|
| Presenter | Antonio Radić (Croatia, no title, no apologies) | IM Levy Rozman (IM 2018, peak FIDE 2421) |
| Est. | 2016 — first chess channel to 1M subs | Full-time content from 2020; teaching a decade prior |
| Subs | ~1.35M, plateaued post-boom | ~8M (crossed 5M mid-2024, still climbing) |
| Format | One game per video, same board, same layout, since forever | Recaps, Guess the Elo, openings, chess news as sports broadcast |

| Weakness | The format never evolves, because evolving it was never the point | The thumbnails run hotter than the content needs; news expires |

**Round 1 — Content Quality.** The chess is not in question on either side; the craft around it is where they split. Levy is a credentialed teacher — International Master, a decade of coaching before the boom, a structured course platform, and a beginners' book good enough to hit the bestseller lists — and it shows in the best of the work: a Gotham opening breakdown teaches a middlegame plan, not just a trap. But the daily engine also ships a lot of content that is news first and chess second, dressed in packaging that has been a running community complaint for years. agadmator's quality curve is flatter and calmer: a great historical game, clearly narrated, allowed to be the star. What he lacks is Levy's instructional depth — you learn what happened, less often how to do it yourself. Teaching beats witnessing, narrowly. *GothamChess wins, on points, with a note from the judges about the thumbnails.*

**Round 2 — Consistency.** Two of the most reliable publishing operations on the entire platform, in any genre. agadmator has uploaded near-daily — often twice daily — for the better part of a decade, through the boom, through the plateau, through the loss of Medo, the chess dog the whole internet mourned with him. Levy's cadence through the boom years was just as unrelenting, at vastly higher production cost per video, while also writing books and running a course business. Choosing between a 96 and a 97 would be false precision. *Draw. Two metronomes, zero excuses.*

**Round 3 — Replay Value.** The archive gets its revenge. A 2022 GothamChess tournament recap is a newspaper — brilliant on the morning it mattered, trivia by Christmas. agadmator's back catalogue is Morphy's Opera Game, Tal dismantling grandmasters like a man late for a bus, Fischer's Game of the Century — evergreen when he filmed them because they had already been evergreen for a hundred years. He didn't build a news desk; he built a library, and libraries appreciate. *agadmator wins, and it isn't close.*

**Round 4 — Community.** Levy's community is enormous, participatory, and load-bearing — Guess the Elo literally runs on viewer-submitted games. But it has the temperature of a stadium: loud, fun, transient. agadmator's comment section is one of the internet's quiet miracles — a decade-old congregation that knows the liturgy, grieved a dog together like a village, and treats the channel less like content than like a local. Scale is a real argument, and it loses to belonging. *agadmator wins.*

**Round 5 — X-Factor.** Two rounds each, one draw, and the fight comes down to the axis it was always coming down to: which channel is irreplaceable? agadmator is chess YouTube's founding institution — but the format he pioneered is, precisely because of its beautiful simplicity, imitable. Levy is the opposite case. When the biggest scandal in chess history broke in September 2022, an entire sport went to one place to find out what had happened, and that place was a YouTube channel run by an IM from New York. That is not a format. That is a *position*: chess's answer to the evening news, self-appointed, boom-forged, and currently without a plausible substitute. You can clone a board layout. You cannot clone being the place everyone already goes. *GothamChess wins the round, and with it the fight.*

**The Decision.** GothamChess, 83–80, and the margin is honest: two rounds apiece with a draw, settled on the axes this magazine weights hardest. He does not win because he is six times bigger; he wins because the boom created a job — chess's broadcaster — and he is the only person on the platform actually holding it. And yet the loser leaves the ring holding the thing the winner should envy: agadmator's 74 Replay Value against Levy's 66 is the quiet headline. When the news cycle finally slows — and it always slows — the library outlasts the broadcast. Levy won the boom. Antonio built the thing the boom was standing on. Both sentences are true; only one of them wins a Boss Fight; the other one wins something slower.

**Post-fight.** Neither enters the [Top 50](/top50/): GothamChess's 83 lands under a cutline that Issue #019 raises to 85, and joins the 83-tier waiting room alongside Eddy Burback; agadmator's 80 is an EXCELLENT that was never going to argue with the arithmetic. Both scores are now the official record; the pairing is retired.
